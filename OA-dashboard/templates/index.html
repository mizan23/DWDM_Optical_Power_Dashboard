📡 Optical Amplifier Power Dashboard

📘 Amplifier Roles & Directions
1 Transmit-end OA (Add Direction)
Booster amplifier at the transmit side.
2 Downstream OA (Drop Direction)
Located at the receiving end (Pre-amplifier).
3 4 Second OA (Pass-through Direction)
Cascaded amplifiers where output of one feeds another.
💡 Used to compensate fiber loss and meet sensitivity requirements.
🔹 1. Transmit-end OA Input Power
P(total) = P(single) + 10 log₁₀(N)
P(total) → Total combined optical power (dBm)
This is what goes into the amplifier after combining multiple channels.
P(single) → Power of a single optical channel (dBm)
Example: one wavelength or one signal.
N → Number of channels/signals being combined
More channels = higher total power.
💡 If you combine multiple signals, power increases logarithmically—not linearly.
🔹 2. Downstream OA Input Power
P(in) = P(pre-out) − (P(pre-single-out) − P(single-in))
P(in) → Input power to the downstream amplifier
P(pre-out) → Total output power from previous stage
P(pre-single-out) → Single-channel output
P(single-in) → Expected input per channel
💡 Adjusts total power based on channel-level differences.
🔹 3. Second OA Input Power
P(second-in) = P(first-out) − (P(first-single-out) − P(second-single-in)) + Offset
Offset → Fiber loss / connectors / margin
💡 Applies real-world corrections and losses.
🔹 4. Mixed Lambda (100G + Beyond)
Convert → Sum → Convert Back
dBm cannot be added directly.
Convert to mW → sum → convert back.
💡 This is how real optical planning tools calculate total power.
